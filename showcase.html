Routing signatures: case studies and statistics

Seven routing laws on the same controlled multi-agent system, then the statistics behind conservation and verification.

1. The seven routing laws

How the rows differ. All seven laws see the same histories, the same frozen executor and the same precommitted key. They differ only in which constraints the key must respect: none (unprojected bias), first-step rewards only (immediate-value), or the full continuation values (RouterMAS). The random feasible row keeps the constraints but ignores the key; the two inert rows show where the carrier vanishes. Columns are maxima over all twenty histories except mean KL.

2. Case studies

What a case shows. Each case is one history of the finite-state system: six legal delegations with their reference probability p, the RouterMAS law qK, the immediate-value law, both continuation values QU and QC, both first-step rewards rU and rC, and the key score sK. The summary rows are computed from the table: the local conservation residual (q − p)⊤Q is what Proposition 1 requires to vanish, and the residual on r is what the immediate-value law makes vanish instead.

Three histories are shown: the one where the key moves the most probability, the one where it moves the least, and the one where matching only first-step rewards leaves the largest continuation drift.

4. Statistical evidence

4.1 Sequential detection

Rejection uses the running maximum of the log evidence across repeated four-step episodes, so a trace group counts as detected if it ever reaches log(1/α) within the budget. The reference false-positive rate stays below α at every budget, as Ville's inequality guarantees for the conditional-action null.

5. What these experiments establish

Established with exact dynamics. Prefix-wise conservation holds to rounding precision while routing changes; a law that matches only first-step rewards leaves downstream drift; row-space keys and full-rank histories are inert; the keyed gain and the expected log evidence follow the shared projection of Corollary 1; the running-maximum likelihood-ratio test keeps its false-positive rate below α while its power grows with the audit budget.

Measured on LLM multi-agent systems. Task success, operating cost, prefix drift DU,95 and fixed-FPR detection on reasoning (GSM8K), code generation (HumanEval, MBPP) and environment interaction (ALFWorld), with centralized, layered and graph-routed routers, are the subject of the evaluation protocol in the extended abstract. Numbers in this volume come only from the controlled systems described above.
